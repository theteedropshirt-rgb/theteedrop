The Heavyweight Difference: Why We Stopped Using Thin 150 GSM Blanks

By The Tee Drop Editorial • November 2, 2026 • 4 min read

When you hold a truly well-made t-shirt, you feel it immediately. The weight, the balance across the shoulders, and the way the fabric falls straight without clinging or twisting sideways.

For years, mass-market apparel has cut corners by printing on lightweight 140–160 GSM blends. They feel okay in the showroom, but after a single machine wash, the collar ripples like bacon and the seams twist diagonally across your torso.

What Does 240 GSM Actually Mean?

GSM stands for grams per square meter. A 240 GSM combed ringspun cotton fabric provides enough structural density to hold an intentional drop-shoulder silhouette. It shields against windy days, sits cleanly over athletic physiques, and provides an ultra-flat printing canvas that allows Direct-to-Garment inks to bond deep into each strand without cracking.

How We Source Our Blanks

Every piece in our drops is spun from long-staple combed cotton, pre-shrunk before cutting, and stitched with reinforced twin-needle hems. It’s apparel made to be worn hundreds of times, not thrown away next season.
